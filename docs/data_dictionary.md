# Data Dictionary — Movie Ticket Booking Analytics (dataset v2)

> **All data is synthetic.** The 10,000 booking records were generated by
> `src/00_generate_data.py` with `random.seed(42)`. Movie titles, multiplex
> names and customers are invented. Nothing in this file describes real
> box-office performance, real cinemas or real people.

## 1. What one row means

**One row = one booking.** A booking can contain several seats (tickets).
This distinction matters throughout the project:

| Term | Definition | How to calculate |
|---|---|---|
| Booking | One transaction / one row | `COUNT(*)` or `COUNT(DISTINCT booking_id)` |
| Ticket | One seat sold | `SUM(seats)` |
| Revenue | Money collected | `SUM(total_amount)` where `booking_status = 'Confirmed'` |
| Customer | One person | `COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id)` |
| Show | One movie at one screen, date and time | `COUNT(DISTINCT show_id)` |
| Occupancy | Share of a show's seats that were sold | `SUM(seats confirmed for show) / seats_capacity` |

Cancelled bookings keep their row (so we can measure cancellations) but are
**excluded from revenue and occupancy**, because the seats were released.

## 5. Business rules baked into the data

1. **Revenue formula:** `total_amount = ticket_price × seats + convenience_fee − discount_amount`.
2. **Promo rates:** WEEKEND10 = 10%, STUDENT15 = 15%, FIRSTBUY5 = 5%, FAMILY20 = 20%, NONE = 0%.
3. **Ticket-type price multipliers:** Standard ×1.0, Premium ×1.4, Recliner ×2.2.
4. **Capacity:** a booking can never sell more seats than the screen holds.
5. **Occupancy:** calculated only from **Confirmed** bookings.
6. **Show consistency:** every booking with the same `show_id` must share the
   same movie, theatre, screen, capacity, date and time.
7. **Customer consistency:** every booking with the same `customer_id` must
   share the same name, gender and home city.
8. **Movie release:** a movie cannot have a show before its release date.

## 6. Known limitations

- **Sample, not a census.** 10,000 bookings is a sample. Only shows with at
  least one sampled booking appear, so occupancy describes those shows, not
  the full annual utilisation of each multiplex. The generator designs 16
  multiplexes × 5 screens = 80 screens; the sample contains **76**, because
  four screens have no sampled booking. This is a property of the sample, not
  a claim that those multiplexes have fewer screens.
- **Occupancy is slightly conservative.** The generator simulated 290 complete
  shows and then randomly kept 10,000 of their bookings. Because a show's
  unsampled seats are invisible, measured occupancy is marginally below the
  designed occupancy (41.23% mean per show vs 42.14% aggregate in the generated
  base).
- **Illustrative city mix.** City volumes are generated from a prior that
  roughly reflects multiplex market size, then sampled. They are not a market
  estimate.
- **Fictional data, real titles.** All figures are synthetic. Four recent
  release names (KGF Chapter 2, Pushpa 2: The Rule, War 2, Animal) are used as
  recognisable labels only; their booking volumes are simulated and say nothing
  about real box-office performance. All venues and people are invented.
- **Designed seasonality.** Monthly patterns (festival/summer peaks) are built
  into the generator, not observed from the real market.
- **Ratings are static.** Each movie has one rating for the whole period; the
  data cannot show how ratings changed over time.
